The Technology Behind

Deep learning models and forensic techniques used in this system

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)

CNNs are the foundation of image analysis in deep learning. Using multiple convolutional layers, pooling, and activation functions, CNNs automatically learn spatial hierarchies of features — from edges and textures to high-level semantic patterns that reveal manipulation artifacts.

Conv LayersMax PoolingReLUSoftmax

ResNet-50 Architecture

Residual Networks (ResNet-50) solve the vanishing gradient problem through skip connections, enabling training of very deep networks. With 50 layers and ~25M parameters, it achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in detecting subtle pixel-level manipulations and GAN artifacts.

Skip Connections50 LayersBatch NormImageNet

Error Level Analysis (ELA)

ELA is a forensic technique that detects regions of an image saved at different compression levels. Authentic images compress uniformly, while edited or spliced regions exhibit anomalous error levels — a powerful signal for localizing manipulation zones.

JPEG ArtifactsCompression MapRegion DetectionForensics

Frequency Domain Analysis

Using Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) and wavelet decomposition, the system analyzes an image's frequency spectrum. GAN-generated images and deepfakes often leave distinctive spectral fingerprints invisible to the human eye but detectable by the model.

FFTWaveletSpectral AnalysisGAN Detection

Transfer Learning

By fine-tuning pretrained models on specialized fake image datasets (FaceForensics++, CASIA, NIST Nimble), the system achieves high accuracy without training from scratch. Transfer learning preserves rich feature representations while adapting to the forensics domain.

Fine-tuningFaceForensics++CASIA DatasetDomain Adaptation
